The map

Freeze the line. The land Russia holds becomes the condominium.

About 19% of Ukraine, 43,000 square miles, from Luhansk to Crimea. Ukraine keeps everything it holds, the fortress belt included. No army withdraws; no city is evacuated.

Annexes

1

Crimea and the fleet: basing rights, as under the 1997 lease (to 2017) and the 2010 extension (to 2042).

2

The Zaporizhzhia plant: international operation, IAEA supervision.

3

Water and the Dnipro line: a technical annex.

If Moscow won’t include Crimea, it goes to a separate protocol.

The trade

A step back at home, a step forward abroad

Moscow

Gives

Its exclusive claim, which only Russia recognizes; the fighting; and a share of the territory’s revenue.

Gets

A status the world accepts, renewed while it keeps the terms. Reconnection with Europe and the West, earned over years of good faith. A breach costs both.

Kyiv

Gives

Exclusive sovereignty on paper, over land it does not control.

Gets

Its defensive line; an end to the fighting; rights and a revenue share inside the territory; its claim intact.

The West

Gives

Recognition, as the price of ending the war, through a resolution that must be renewed. Reconnection. Both only while Russia performs.

Gets

A settlement Kyiv can sign, and a lesson for the next war: conquest doesn’t pay.

Recognition ends the war, and lasts only while Russia keeps the terms. Reconnection is earned, for decades. A war of conquest must not pay.

THE TRADE

SHEET 7 OF 18 · X.COM/THEKINGDAVIDJR

Both constitutions

Nothing is ceded, so nothing breaks

Russia · Article 67(2.1), since 2020

“Actions aimed at alienating part of the territory of the Russian Federation … are not permitted.”

A condominium alienates nothing. Russia renounces no claim, and the treaty never says the land is anyone else’s.

Ukraine · Article 73

“Issues of altering the territory of Ukraine are resolved exclusively by an All-Ukrainian referendum.”

Nothing is altered on paper, and the referendum this proposal requires is the route the constitution prescribes.

Ukraine can’t hold a referendum under martial law, so its vote follows the ceasefire.

Precedents

1

The Timor Gap Treaty, 1989: Australia and Indonesia split the central zone’s revenue equally without agreeing a border; from 2002, 90:10 for East Timor.

2

The Saudi–Kuwaiti Neutral Zone: oil revenue shared equally for decades.

Recognition is what lets the territory’s coal, steel and electricity sell legally at market prices, so Russia’s share of a legal economy can be worth more than all of a sanctioned one.

A strike on the territory, or a breach of its terms, costs the side responsible its share.

THE REVENUE

Why the name matters

A name is a specification

“There are two hard problems in computer science: cache invalidation and naming things.”

1

It’s told as a joke, but a name says what a thing is for before the thing exists.

2

The proposals on the table use bureaucratic language: demilitarized zones, civilian administrations, free economic zones. That gives neither side a story to tell its own people.

3

Each country ratifies by plebiscite, so the name is what each electorate will understand itself to be voting on.

THE NAME

SHEET 10 OF 18 · X.COM/THEKINGDAVIDJR

The Kyivan Rus Condominium

A name that gives each side something

Kyivan Rus was the medieval state, centered on Kyiv, from which both Ukrainians and Russians trace their origins.

For Moscow

Putin justified the war in civilizational terms: historical unity, one people, a shared cradle. A settlement named after Rus lets him finish that story.

Its strongest ground

By the Primary Chronicle’s account, Volodymyr of Kyiv was baptized at Chersonesus, in Crimea, in 988. Putin has called Crimea Russia’s Temple Mount: a place more than one faith holds sacred.

For Kyiv

Russia’s school history standard dropped “Kievan Rus” for “the state of Rus.” So “Kyivan” in the treaty is a real concession from Moscow, and a permanent marker of Kyiv’s claim on land Russia runs.

THE OTHER MAP

SHEET 13 OF 18 · X.COM/THEKINGDAVIDJR

What a name can’t do

Two kinds of guarantee

1 · Ukraine itself

The U.S., Europe and NATO

Already under negotiation, and the main subject of the Miami talks. They should rest on a treaty, not on any one government’s goodwill. A condominium doesn’t replace them; it depends on them.

2 · The condominium

Monitors and arbiters

From states both sides can accept. The UAE, which has hosted trilateral talks and prisoner exchanges, is an obvious candidate; Turkey, India and Serbia are further possibilities. A naive first pass.

Without credible guarantors, any condominium becomes what Minsk was: a piece of paper that buys time for the next invasion.

What this proposal doesn’t do

Limits, stated up front

1

If Putin values “we took it” more than the world’s acceptance, the trade fails. Russia has lived with the non-recognition of Crimea since 2014.

2

Covering the whole line turns a Donbas proposal into a settlement of the war, a larger claim than one essay can carry.

3

It doesn’t settle how enforcement would work, or how Ukraine’s rights inside the condominium would be protected in practice.

I’m a security engineer, not a historian of the Slavic world; I expect specialists to refine the history. The argument depends on one observation: the compromise has a mechanism but no name.
